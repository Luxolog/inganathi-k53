Rules of the Road

Know what a driver may, must and must not do on a public road.

Speed & conditions

  • Know the applicable speed limit and obey the displayed limit.
  • Reduce speed when road, weather, traffic or visibility conditions require it.
  • Your document identifies 60 km/h as the general urban-area speed and 120 km/h as the maximum freeway speed in the material.

Following distance

Keep enough space to stop safely if the vehicle ahead slows or stops. Your source teaches the two-second method: choose a fixed point, wait for the vehicle ahead to pass it, then count “1001, 1002”.

Overtaking

  • Do not overtake where visibility is poor.
  • Do not overtake where doing so could create a hazard for approaching traffic.
  • Do not pass a vehicle stopped at a pedestrian crossing.
  • If another driver is overtaking you, keep as far left as practicable and do not accelerate.

Changing lanes

Changing lanes is only safe when the way is clear. Signal your intention, use mirrors, observe surrounding traffic and check the blind spot before moving.

Memory: Mirrors → Signal → Observe → Blind spot → Move.

Intersections

  • Approach every intersection prepared to respond to traffic and signs.
  • At a four-way stop, stop as required and proceed only when it is safe.
  • When turning, signal in good time and watch pedestrians and other road users.

Traffic circles

Yield according to the applicable traffic rules and road signs. Your source specifically teaches observation toward the right before entering and a blind-spot check, then observation and a left signal before exiting.

Pedestrians

Drivers must pay particular attention at pedestrian crossings. Your source states that a driver must yield right of way to a pedestrian crossing within a pedestrian crossing.

Emergency vehicles

When you become aware of an emergency vehicle sounding a siren, give it right of way when safe to do so. Do not create another hazard while making room.

Mobile phones

Your source warns against holding a cellphone while the vehicle is moving. Even hands-free conversations can distract the driver; the safest choice is to stop legally and safely before dealing with a call.

Accidents

Stop and assess the situation. Your source teaches that accidents should be reported within 24 hours and that, where there are injuries, vehicles should not be moved until instructed by police or traffic officers.

Towing

  • Your source teaches that a vehicle with defective brakes should not be towed with a rope.
  • Use appropriate towing equipment.
  • When towing with a rope, the source gives 30 km/h as the maximum speed.
  • The source also gives 3.5 m as the maximum distance between vehicles when towing each other.

Seat belts & passengers

Seat belts must be worn as required. Your source places responsibility for children under 14 on the driver, while adults are responsible for themselves.

Road Traffic Signs

Learn the meaning and required action — do not rely only on the shape.

Regulatory / command signs

These control what road users must or must not do. Examples in the official sign material include minimum speed, mass restrictions, keep-left/keep-right and direction-only signs. The action shown by the sign must be followed.

Warning signs

Warning signs alert you to hazards or changes ahead. Learn the hazard, not merely the picture: the correct response may be to slow down, prepare to stop, change position or increase observation.

Information signs

Information signs help road users identify routes, destinations, facilities and other useful road information. Learn what information the sign provides and how it affects your journey.

Temporary signs

Your source emphasises that temporary signs indicate that circumstances on the roadway are not normal and that temporary signs have the same legal significance as permanent signs.

Remember: Temporary does not mean optional.
Study tip When you see a sign in the test, ask: What is the sign called? What does it mean? What action must I take?

Road Markings & Traffic Signals

Read the road itself — lines and markings can control movement.

Centre and lane lines

Learn to distinguish lines that separate opposing traffic from lines separating lanes travelling in the same direction. Always consider the sign, road marking and surrounding traffic together.

Barrier / solid lines

Where a barrier line or other restriction prevents a manoeuvre, do not cross it simply because the road appears clear. Your question bank tests recognition of these markings.

Stop lines

Where a stop sign or stop control applies, stop at the appropriate stop line before proceeding when it is safe.

Yield markings

Approach a yield control at a speed that allows you to observe other traffic and give way as required before proceeding.

Pedestrian crossings

Watch for pedestrians and vehicles that have stopped at a crossing. Your source specifically tests the prohibition on passing a vehicle stopped at a pedestrian crossing.

Arrows & painted areas

Lane arrows and painted islands can direct traffic or separate traffic streams. Do not drive through an area where the marking or sign requires you to keep clear.

Vehicle Controls

Know what every major control does and why it matters for safe driving.

↔

Steering wheel

Controls direction. Your source recommends keeping both hands on the wheel as far as possible, using the “quarter to three” or “ten to two” position.

＋

Accelerator

Controls engine power and allows the vehicle's speed to increase or decrease.

■

Service brake

Used to slow or stop the vehicle. Emergency braking requires firm control and awareness of the vehicle and road conditions.

C

Clutch

Disconnects the engine from the transmission / gearbox, as stated in your source material.

R

Gear lever

Selects the required transmission gear. Use the appropriate gear for the situation.

P

Parking brake

Helps keep a parked vehicle stationary. Your source also refers to applying the parking brake when stopped at the side of the road.

◉

Mirrors

Allow the driver to monitor traffic behind and around the vehicle. Mirror observation is essential before manoeuvres.

↪

Indicators

Signal your intention to other road users. Keep the signal displayed for long enough to communicate the manoeuvre clearly.

☀

Headlights

Use lighting appropriate to the conditions and visibility. Your source specifically tests when headlights are required because visibility is reduced.

!

Hazard lights

Warn other road users of a hazard or stopped vehicle where appropriate. Do not use them as a substitute for normal signalling.

W

Windscreen wipers

Maintain visibility during rain or other conditions that reduce the driver's view.

H

Hooter

Your source teaches that the hooter must not be used unnecessarily and may be used to help avoid a crash.

Emergency stop — remember the sequence

Keep both hands firmly on the steering wheel. Check behind you. Brake hard but progressively without locking the wheels. Do not apply the parking brake until the vehicle has stopped.

5

Hazard Awareness

Good drivers identify hazards before they become emergencies.

Moving hazards

Your source identifies vehicles, people and animals as examples of moving hazards. Their position and behaviour can change quickly.

Fixed hazards

Road layout, intersections, bends, signs, traffic islands and other permanent features can create hazards that should be anticipated before you reach them.

Changing hazards

Your source identifies weather and the road surface as changing or variable hazards. Rain, poor visibility or a slippery surface can increase stopping distance.

Wet weather

Drive more slowly when conditions require it and increase the following distance. Be prepared for reduced grip and visibility.

Blind spots

Mirrors do not show every area around the vehicle. Before changing lanes or moving laterally, check the blind spot as part of your observation.

Think ahead

Do not only react to what is directly in front of you. Scan for developing hazards, traffic behaviour, pedestrians, road signs and changes in road conditions.

Quick Revision

High-value facts drawn from the supplied Inganathi source material. Verify any administrative detail with your DLTC before relying on it.

Core test areas

Rules of the road, road traffic signs and vehicle controls are the three official core study areas for the learner's licence test.

Urban speed

Your supplied questionnaire uses 60 km/h as the general urban-area speed. Always obey the actual applicable posted limit.

Freeway speed

Your supplied questionnaire uses 120 km/h as the maximum legal freeway speed. Always obey the applicable sign and conditions.

Two-second rule

Use a fixed roadside point and count “1001, 1002” after the vehicle ahead passes it. If you reach the point before finishing, increase the gap.

Emergency vehicle

Give right of way to an emergency vehicle sounding a siren when it is safe to do so.

Temporary signs

Temporary signs indicate abnormal road circumstances and have the same legal significance as permanent signs according to your source material.

Cellphone

Your source prohibits holding a cellphone while the vehicle is moving and warns that even hands-free conversation can distract you.

Seat belts

Seat belts must be worn as required. Your source places responsibility for children under 14 on the driver and adults on themselves.

Before a manoeuvre

Observe, assess, signal, check mirrors and blind spots, then proceed only when safe.

Important source noteThis study manual is educational preparation, not a legal interpretation of the National Road Traffic Act.
